# Fitme stop-word list: English (A§7, AGENTS.md §2 "Stop words halt the session").
#
# One term per line. Blank lines are ignored. A line starting with `#` is a comment; a line
# of the exact form `# category: <name>` starts a new category for every term that follows
# it, until the next such header. A line ending in `*` is a *stem*: it matches as a prefix of
# any word in the text, not as an exact phrase.
#
# Chest/heart co-occurrence is handled in code, not here (`guards/stop_words/__init__.py`,
# `_chest_or_heart_hit`, A§7.1): a chest/heart token halts only together with a symptom
# descriptor in the same clause ("my chest feels tight"), never on its own ("chest day",
# "chest press 3x8"). Only explicit cardiac terms that halt on their own belong in this file's
# chest_or_heart category.
#
# Matching is deliberately broad (A§7.1): "no pain today" MUST halt. That is an accepted
# cost, not a bug — the guard has to be deterministic and impossible to argue around, and the
# precheck/check-in flows use buttons rather than free text, which keeps false positives rare
# in practice.
#
# Ordinary muscle soreness/fatigue words are intentionally excluded: normal DOMS after a hard
# session ("sore", "stiff", "tight hamstrings") must not halt training, and neither do
# cramps, bare "weak"/"tired", or exertional breathlessness ("out of breath", "can't catch my
# breath").
#
# Where a stem collides with plan-editing language, a phrase is used instead (A§7.1):
# "tweaked my" rather than `tweak*` ("tweak the plan"), "heard a crack" rather than `crack*`
# ("cracked the plateau"). `strain*` and `tender` are kept as they are: "strain gauge" and
# "tender loving care" halting is an accepted cost.
